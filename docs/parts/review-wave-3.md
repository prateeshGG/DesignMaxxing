## 2. Already consistent (founder to confirm)

- **Table ownership:** 2.3 and 2.4 own no table; 2.5 owns `capture_job_change` and `crawler_presence` and writes `crawl_run` and `capture_job` rows; 2.10 owns six tables. No table has two owners. Names follow the snake_case singular convention.
- **The child never touches storage or the database:** 2.3 and 2.4 write files into `/batch` only; the derive step is outside the child (2.4); the gate reads stored evidence and sends no request to any site.
- **One definition of section bounds:** DOM-measured, CSS pixels, page coordinates (2.3 `SectionMap.bounds`), mapped to 1.2's `section.bounds_*`, read by 2.4 (R15) and 2.10 (G12, G12b, G12c).
- **Pass names:** `motion` / `static` are the same in 1.2, 2.2, 2.3, 2.10 (the manifest field is `pass`; 1.2's column is `capture_pass`).
- **Time:** attempt 90 s, heartbeat 10 s, lease 60 s are the same in 1.4, 2.2, 2.3, 2.4 and 2.9. Every clock in 2.5 is the Postgres clock.
- **F-45 and F-46** are applied in all four specs (crawler machine; sites are chosen by a person; 2.5 never adds a source; 2.3 never follows a link; 2.4 never fetches what the page did not ask for).
- **Policy is checked late:** run creation, admission and janitor (2.5), each lease (1.4), the gate and `publishBatch` (2.10), the derive step (2.4 R33).
- **Verdicts:** 2.10 uses 1.4's `gate_verdict` values and reason codes (now including `GATE_REVIEW`); 2.5's roll-up counts `NEEDS_REVIEW` as not good for `crawl` and `recrawl` (F-32 70% rule).
- **Takedown and breakers are never evaded:** 2.5 R13, 2.4 R9 and R28, 2.10 R12 follow DC-08.
- **No new HTTP `/internal/*` endpoint** in any wave-3 spec (F-24).

## 5. Founder decisions from wave 3

1. **Should V0 make playable versions of animations, or only still pictures?** For each animated thing on a site (videos, GIFs, animated icons) we keep the original file, and we can also make a small still preview and a cleaned-up playable video. The playable video for videos and GIFs is easy; for animated icons it needs extra tooling. Nothing is shown to users either way (third-party files stay internal). *Suggested answer: V0 makes the still preview for everything and the playable video only for videos and GIFs; animated icons keep their original file and a still, and get a playable version later if you want it.* (OQ-2.4-3, OQ-2.4-4)
2. **How do approved pages go live to users?** Pages that pass the checks wait in a batch. You would look at a random 5% of each batch (about 21 minutes for the first 250 pages) and press publish. Later this could happen automatically. *Suggested answer: manual batches with the 5% check at first; switch to automatic after three batches in a row, each with at least 100 checked pages, where you found nothing wrong. One bad page holds the whole batch until you decide.* (OQ-2.10-25)
3. **Which browser should take the pictures?** The test browser we use cannot play many of the videos on websites, so hero videos may show as black or as a still. Google Chrome can play them. *Suggested answer: capture with Google Chrome on your computer, and let the 20-site test show how many sites it fixes before we lock it in.* (OQ-2.2-34, OQ-2.3-25)
4. **When is a site "done"?** A site counts as finished when at least 70% of its pages are fully approved. Pages that wait for your review do not count yet, and pages that simply do not exist (a deleted page, a PDF, a page that sends us elsewhere) are ignored. *Suggested answer: yes. If you later approve the waiting pages, the site's result improves automatically and is never made worse.* (OQ-2.5-3, OQ-2.5-4)
5. **How long do we keep the pictures and videos we download from websites?** They stay private, for checking only. They cost storage and are the main thing a site owner could ask us to remove. *Suggested answer: keep the downloaded originals like all other raw evidence, delete the small previews we make from them first if space gets tight, and measure the real size in the 20-site test before deciding anything stricter.* (OQ-1.3-22, OQ-1.3-23)
6. **If a website blocks us, should the crawler stay switched on while that site waits for its next try?** After a block the site rests for 6 hours before one careful test visit. For the crawler to make that test, the crawl must still be running; otherwise the site waits until you start the crawler again. *Suggested answer: stay on while a blocked site waits (the other sites carry on, and you can press stop at any time).* (OQ-2.5-5, OQ-2.5-11)
